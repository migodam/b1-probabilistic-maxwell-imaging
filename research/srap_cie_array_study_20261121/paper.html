Receiver-weak, propagation-strong current updates in limited-aperture Maxwell inversion

Abstract

A twofold current decomposition identifies modes that couple weakly to receivers while propagating strongly inside a scattering region. We test whether an alternating field–current projection in this space improves a sourcewise, dual-parameter contraction integral equation (Dual-CIE) solver. The update is followed by a bound-constrained shared-material projection and a legal Fourier current injection. We compared cold-start, wall-limited reconstructions on 24 independent two-dimensional materials at seven matched channel counts, eight further continuous and thin-bridge materials, twelve three-dimensional vector materials at two array geometries, and three experimental Fresnel objects. At the standard 180° arrangement, 21/24 raw comparisons favored routing (median relative L2 reduction 39.1%), yet 18 of those improvements accompanied increased error inside the physical object. On the additional eight materials the entire half-aperture cohort had lower material error, but three raw reconstructions had zero half-contrast overlap. In the 3D hemisphere the median absolute paired L2 gain after 180 s correction was 0.0113 (95% paired interval 0.0024–0.0243); the planar array had zero median half-contrast IoU under both methods. In measured 8 GHz Twin data the better of the two geometries produced two localized features with ordinary Dual-CIE, whereas routing produced disjoint speckles after correction. These results bound a geometry-dependent benefit and identify distinct contributions of background suppression, current locking, time-to-stage, and true material localization.

Introduction

Finite-aperture scattering measurements determine some induced-current components far more accurately than others. In high contrast, an unobserved current can nevertheless modify the total field that excites other material cells. A limited-aperture pilot suggested that projecting joint field and current changes onto this population could improve reconstructions. A previous three-stage pilot reported nine paired gains in eleven exposed settings. A four-stage run on a different machine had two raw gains. Both the algorithmic schedule and elapsed-time throughput differed. Here we remove the dense Green material denominator, retain a sourcewise dual-parameter CIE, match each receiver arrangement across solvers, and test the physical mechanism directly.

Related work

Subspace optimization methods iteratively correct ambiguous current components after resolving a deterministic part from the receiving operator. Twofold SOM further uses interior propagation. The NIE and CIE families use material-dependent transformations to improve high-contrast scattering inversion, including dual-parameter and Fourier-restricted updates. Learned CIE-SOM and multiresolution CIE already address limited aperture or computational resources. Our experimental question concerns the effect of a specific projected finite current–field update inside this existing family; no new general alternating-projection convergence theorem is claimed. Method

Write the physical finite system as Q=Eᶦⁿᶜ+GJ, J=diag(m)Q, and Y=SJ+η. For vector fields each scalar material acts on the three electric components of all illuminations. Contrast is real, bounded and isotropic. The baseline alternates sourcewise PR current steps with scalar CIE material readout and a frozen Fourier/β continuation. Let x=J/6, B=6G and let P be the strong receiving subspace at relative singular threshold 0.1. Let Π be the projector onto eigenmodes of (I−P)G*G(I−P) with energy above 0.01‖G‖². Source-specific sampling masks enter the receiver Gram; illumination is kept in each state.

Every 16 completed PR steps, or at a stage transition, the routed method projects the current and field with their stored differences onto the real shared-material set, then onto the affine propagation graph intersected with a common measured-data ball. Differences from the current graph state are projected by Π, separately on current and field. A second shared-material projection gives a physical material proposal. The associated current is fitted to the currently legal Fourier/DPCS representation and PR history is reset. Audit and endpoint selection use the original Maxwell forward map. Raw and short box-constrained physical correction are reported at the same wall limits. The correction branch starts from a completed audit in the first 80% of the time window and uses at most 20 accepted steps.

Mechanism and implementation

For a graph displacement (d,Bd), the routed intervention incurs the exact finite mismatch e=(ΠB−BΠ)d. It vanishes if routing and propagation commute on this step. A graph-compatible control retains the same current direction and uses BΠd for the field. It can change the shared material selected next. Four interventions started from identical complete sourcewise states, including PR history and fields; at eight subsequent CIE steps the lowest training residual was achieved by a PR reset, full AP, full AP, and continued Dual, respectively. A smaller receiver perturbation alone did not rank these interventions.

There is a second loss after projection. For U=(I−VV)F and the material-projected target change t, the energy of the discarded part splits orthogonally into ‖Vt‖² and ‖(I−VV*−UU†)t‖². Across the four saved states, 54.6–66.7% of the discarded energy was in the locked DPCS space. For the Fresnel state, the routed current had a negligible locked fraction before the material projection, but the material-projected change already contained 18.39% locked energy. A physical constitutive map can rotate apparently unobservable updates into observable directions.

The Euclidean graph projection solves with H=I+B*B. FFT-preconditioned CG replaces the interior dense Cholesky in 2D; source-specific, small port Schur factors enforce the single data-ball multiplier. At one 60² measured state, the unconstrained graph projection already satisfied the ball. Deferring port setup reduced the first projection from 17.49 to 1.45 s, preserving the returned fields. When the bound is active, the implementation caches all required factors. The vector graph uses FFT-CG as well, while the thresholded three-dimensional routing spectrum is computed by a dense 5,184-dimensional factorization. Implementation and costs therefore depend on the role and dimension of the operator.

Experiments

The primary 2D study uses a 400 MHz full-space scalar TM model on a 2 m region. We generated the noisy fields on a 96² mesh and retrieved 32² independent real contrasts on the inverse grid. Each of 24 random materials was observed under seven 10-transmitter, 20-receiver arrangements. Plane-wave illumination and finite cylindrical sources have separate arm-matched reference geometries. A follow-up uses eight new physical materials, relates narrower angular windows with fixed orientation, and reverses Tx with S fixed. Each 2D arrangement has independent fixed-norm 10% complex noise. Each object and arm pair was run on one host. The 24 materials are the primary sampling units; reported 95% paired intervals use resampling of objects within geometry. Synthetic and measured responses were never pooled.

The three-dimensional study pairs a rectangular planar transmitter/receiver grid and a hemispherical array on 12 additional materials, with two dipole polarizations, 16 incident states and 32 measured components. A dyadic full-wave solver generated 24³ data; inversion used 12³ material unknowns. The arrays share the smallest distance to the image center and dipole moment magnitudes, with different angular coverage and distributions of ranges. Original Maxwell audits, offline fine-grid truth averaged to the inverse grid, noise and a dielectric sphere benchmark constrain interpretation. Measured Fresnel results are evaluated by held-out complex fields; voxel scores are undefined for those acquisitions.

Results

The main 180° comparison gave 21 wins, one tie and two losses in raw L2 at 30 s, with paired median reduction 39.1% (95% interval 27.2–47.3%); the corrected comparison gave 22 wins and two losses, reduction 31.9% (23.4–37.9%). Median raw background error fell from 1.768 to 0.523. In 19 of the 21 total-error wins there was no increase in half-contrast overlap, and in 18 the foreground error was higher. Corrected images of three-body cases 003/013, double ring 010, and core/shell 018 showed genuine recovery. Case 007 exposed a reversal: at full aperture the raw gain was 80.1%, whereas the standard half-aperture raw error rose by 481.0%. In the latter case both methods generated exactly the same first-step box-saturated material. SRAP retained it because all subsequently audited states within budget had higher physical objective. For the source-matched straight and radial receive arrays, shared singular-value truncation moved the algorithms away from raw air, while median high-contrast overlap on the same-side line and matching arc remained zero.

The supplement separates material localization from background suppression. In Cx06 the two bright centers and their weaker host are resolved, and the light-corrected overlap rises from 0.156 to 0.838. In Cx04 both endpoints of a thin bridge are localized, but their connection is lost even as relative error falls from 2.301 to 0.824. On Cx01 the Y-shaped connectivity is lost. Across eight materials the median raw half-aperture relative error reduction was 51.7%, falling to 13.3% after reversing the illumination with the same receiver matrix. Independent observation noise accompanies each placement. Aligning the nominal 120° center with the half aperture did not restore the rank-15 raw solutions. Conditioning of the locked receiving subspace provides a quantitative diagnostic. With 15 singular directions the normalized RMS DPCS noise gain was 0.96, 8.05, and 380 at the three plane-wave apertures and about 9,179 on straight receiver lines.

For the 12 hemisphere acquisitions at 180 s the raw and corrected error favored routing on 11 and 9 objects, respectively. Median corrected IoU increased from 0.430 to 0.488. At 60 s the corrected paired absolute L2 gain was −0.0147 with an interval crossing zero. On the planar grid both methods fitted the noisy field near its 10% scale despite zero median half-peak IoU. All methods missed the closed cavity in shell 00. Neither method reached the true half-peak on the three separated-body objects. The nominal 180 s ceiling left substantial unused time after convergence: median raw execution was 6.81 s for Dual versus 39.96 s for SRAP, across both arrays. The sphere complex far-field discrepancy decreased from 7.95% to 6.15% upon refinement. A 36³ check of one shell measured 24³-to-36³ complex-port differences of 1.92% and 1.71% on the two arrays.

In paired 30 s Mac runs, lazy port setup let SRAP execute more auxiliary updates across all eight measured arrangements. For the Twin phantom at 6 GHz, withheld-transmitter error after light correction was 0.141 for lazy SRAP and 0.162 for Dual. At 8 GHz, interleaved sampling of the same number of channels gave 0.798 and 0.257, respectively, with localized inclusions for Dual and fragmented features for SRAP. The same 733-channel contiguous array produced errors of 1.025 and 1.001 on the common withheld transmitters. Tested masks and frequencies belong to three physical objects.

Discussion and limitations

The original objective can prefer a low-amplitude estimate in an ambiguous or poorly represented aperture. Relative material L2 rewards elimination of a large spurious background even when a hole or bridge is missing. The effect of a new step thus has three observable components: the material path, its competition with elapsed time and physical auditing, and the spatial origin of the material error. A nonconvex shared-material projection and Fourier reinjection also preclude generalizing nonexpansiveness of a linear orthogonal projector to the complete iteration. The replay on two machines establishes deployment sensitivity. Independent 2D array noise limits causal attribution to array orientation alone. Three-dimensional evidence is finite-discretization evidence at moderate electrical size; real-data arrays have calibration error and incomplete field coverage. A paired interval by geometry does not grant simultaneous coverage across all layouts.

Conclusion

Periodic routing provides reproducible half-aperture background suppression and several genuine morphological recoveries. On planar vector data the structural gain is unresolved; hemisphere results support a small benefit under a longer budget. Held-out experimental data favor ordinary Dual-CIE on the most informative 8 GHz Twin layout. The evidence supports a computational mechanism study, and provides limited support for a general replacement of mature CIE. An actionable engineering result is to test the unconstrained projection against the physical noise ball before paying for active-port factors. The scientific contribution is a falsifiable account of where receiver locking and shared-material projection alter available current directions, with counterexamples at fixed channel counts and independently scored vector and measured data.

A qualification on nonexpansiveness

Consider one cell with x=(1,0), q=(s,√(1−s²)), and real material slope α∈[0,b] with finite b>0. Eliminating the shared field gives the exact projection cost 1−2sα/(1+α²). For negative s its minimizer is 0, for positive s it is min(1,b), and at zero all slopes tie. Arbitrarily close inputs can lead to separated projected states. The linear router is orthogonal; the full nonlinear iteration inherits no global nonexpansive bound from it. This elementary example pinpoints the distinction already relevant to prior shared-material branch studies and supplies no claim that every failing trajectory encounters such a tie.
