Dear Editors of *Inverse Problems*,

We submit for consideration “Material contrast bounds from finite-aperture electromagnetic scattering data.” A finite microwave array may give little information about where the peak dielectric contrast occurs. We ask whether its complex readings can nevertheless rule out every material below a proposed peak, throughout the full search region and at a specified noise level.

The analysis starts from the volume integral equation and the requirement that all illuminations share one material at each point. A matrix inequality tracks which measured source combinations can be lost to negative directions of the current quadratic. The local constitutive equation limits this loss and yields a converse for a whole family of spatial corrections. In a lossless problem with 9,216 independent contrasts, the cap of one remains undecided on all six targets. Even here, the data give a strictly positive lower bound on the current each possible fit must carry in a particular thirteen-dimensional space. This distinguishes a limitation of the calculation from a property of every physically possible reconstruction.

When the loss angle is specified, the same currents obey a dissipative bound in receiver space. On identical full-wave data we bound the performance of every common dissipative penalty and show that independent penalties on coherent illuminations can rule out a cap the common penalty misses. We also show how to reserve part of the illumination space for the shared material constraint and improve the attained bound on a compact scatterer. We give both small-domain successes and large-domain failures, and quantify the loaded-scene error allowance required to apply the result to measurements from two microwave chambers. The anonymous manuscript and accompanying numerical material document the physical assumptions, evidence and limitations.
