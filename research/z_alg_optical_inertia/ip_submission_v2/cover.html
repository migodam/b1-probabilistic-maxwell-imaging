Dear Editors of Inverse Problems,

Please consider “Certifying peak dielectric contrast from finite-aperture scattering data: optical power and negative inertia across illuminations” as a research paper. A low-contrast reconstruction with a large residual leaves open whether a different material with the same peak can fit the data. Our method can exclude the entire low-contrast material box without reconstructing a candidate image.

We show that the matrix sector and optical balances characterize the shared lossless constitutive law when imposed at each pixel on the actual currents. Summing these balances gives a spatial hierarchy. Combining their weighted forms with the measured residual bounds the effect of negative state directions on the illumination spectrum. On two 9,216-variable full-ring cases at 2% noise, the joint bound certifies peak contrast above 0.75. A four-region single-source semidefinite relaxation gives exact receiver fits for every coherent input, under any uniform current-energy cap valid throughout the material box. The proof combines finite-energy moment witnesses with a verified physical current inside the same box. A verified sixteen-region profile certifies the same contrast cap for one coherent input in each case.

We tested the transfer of fixed multipliers on 29 further simulated materials. The joint test excluded peak contrast at most 0.5 on all 22 objects in one archive and at most 5 on five of seven objects in the other. Measurements from Institut Fresnel and the University of Manitoba quantify the additional model discrepancy that the data can tolerate under stated calibration assumptions.

We request double-anonymous review. Thank you for considering the manuscript.
