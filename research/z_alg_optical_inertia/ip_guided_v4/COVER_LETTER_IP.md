Dear Editors of *Inverse Problems*,

Please consider “Which material distributions can finite-aperture scattering data rule out?” for publication as a research paper. We study what a finite set of noisy complex fields can establish about an entire class of dielectric distributions, including distributions that a full-wave optimizer has never visited.

The shared constitutive equation about an arbitrary spatial background yields a measured-field distance bound through an inertia count at the error tolerance. It applies even at a singular current block. A row-weight rule transfers a separating quadratic between nonconcentric nested intervals, and a local change of interval endpoints admits a low-rank update with a fully uncertain exterior. In a 9,216-cell transverse-magnetic model the same support admits a peak-0.625 material and excludes a family centred at peak four. Its verified width exceeds the usual norm-contraction cutoff by a factor of 5.14. A legal perturbation has spectral radius above one.

The experiments include 28 large-domain targets, 12 structured candidate pools, signed samples from a trained flow on its original array, and measurements of seven physical objects in two facilities. The paper reports when extrapolation to an unknown background fails and how much full-wave refinement a box test actually saves. Sorting individual predictions remains faster when a single physically compatible reconstruction suffices. We believe this distinction between material-class identifiability and solver performance will interest readers working on nonlinear inverse scattering and uncertainty quantification.

Yours sincerely,

The authors
