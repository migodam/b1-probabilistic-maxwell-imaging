Cover letter

Dear Editors of Inverse Problems,

Please consider “Lower bounds on peak dielectric contrast from finite-aperture scattering data” as a research paper. We ask whether complex field measurements can rule out an entire range of dielectric contrasts before any image is reconstructed. An unobserved current can carry radiated power outside a partial aperture. Several incident fields place a further restriction on these currents because they must all arise in the same material.

We derive a lower bound on the data distance from a weighted current operator and a measured illumination spectrum. On two 9,216-cell examples, the joint reading rules out peak contrast at most 0.75, while every coherent input admits a zero-residual fit under four regional power balances and a physical current-energy limit. At sixteen regions, a single coherent combination recovers the exclusion. At fixed incident fluence, increasing the number of angles from ten to twenty-four raises the full-ring count from two to five of six scatterers at 2% noise. Independently computed angular responses explain the subsequent plateau. The negative current directions are highly receiver-invisible and have large wave-equation defects in the uniform capped medium.

Additional simulations cover 29 materials on two arrays. Measurements from Institut Fresnel and the University of Manitoba quantify the field-model error still allowable after empirical calibration checks. A near-noise inverse example illustrates the distinction between ruling out a whole class and recovering a more accurate image. Ordinary discrepancy continuation obtains the same material result under the matched comparison.

We request double-anonymous review. Thank you for considering the manuscript.
