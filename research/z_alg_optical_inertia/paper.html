Can joint illuminations certify a material contrast that every scalar single-illumination relaxation misses?

Abstract

Finite-aperture electromagnetic measurements can constrain the maximum dielectric contrast even when a material image remains poorly determined. We consider a lossless scalar transverse-magnetic volume-integral discretization and a prescribed bound on the Frobenius norm of the complex data error. A material sector inequality and an optical power identity hold on the full illumination space. Their weighted combination gives an explicit residual bound which can remain positive in the presence of negative state directions. We construct complementary witnesses, one in the receiver null space and one fitting the data, that cover the entire ratio of the optical and sector multipliers. On two distinct 9,216-cell media the witnesses defeat every globally scalar single-illumination relaxation, including arbitrary coherent illumination choices, while the joint bound strictly excludes peak contrast at most 0.75. Both underlying true media are verified feasible in their 2% noise balls with a small allowance for the forward solve. A half-aperture study on six new media excludes peak contrast at most 0.5 in all six at 2% noise and in three at 10%, including one additional case certified by spatial optical weights. Regional localization fails on 112 tested regions. We quantify the gain and model-error budgets required to transfer a lower bound to measured microwave data.

1. Inverse problem and contribution

Let the measured array be \(Y\) and write \(J=M(B+GJ)\) for all incident fields, with an unknown real diagonal contrast \(0\le M\le U\). The question is whether any such \(M\) obeys \(\|SJ-Y\|_F\le r\). For \(U=uI\), an exclusion proves that every admissible object fitting the data has peak contrast exceeding \(u\). This conclusion remains meaningful when the support cannot be localized. We use every available measured complex field in the declared source groups, with an explicit treatment of missing receiver angles.

The contribution is an inverse-data separation result between a specified family of scalar tests and a shared-illumination test. Prior results already supply lossless field bounds, joint real/reactive power constraints, domain projections, cross-excitation identities, and extremal inertia formulas. A view of these antecedents is necessary to delimit the claim. Proposition-level comparison and primary sources

2. Noisy multi-illumination certificate

For nonnegative diagonal \(D_\mu\) and real diagonal \(D_\lambda\), set \(Z=(D_\mu U+iD_\lambda)/2\), \(K=S^*S+D_\mu-ZG-G^*Z^*\), \(L=S^*Y+ZB\). Let \(\nu=n_-(K)\) and let \(h_j\) be the descending eigenvalues of \(Y^*Y-L^*K^{-1}L\) for nonsingular \(K\). Then every admissible material satisfies

\[\|SJ-Y\|_F^2\ \ge\ \sum_{j>\nu}(h_j)_+.\]

The proof completes the square after adding the sector inequality and the lossless power identity to the residual Gram. A congruence can cancel at most \(\nu\) positive directions; the residual remains positive semidefinite. The bound is sharp for the resulting free Gram epigraph with one weighted constraint. This does not assert sharpness over physical media. Source-dependent masks are accommodated by disjoint groups with common receivers. A block-matrix, threshold-count formulation covers the singular case. Detailed proofs

3. Why adding the optical identity can help, and why it can fail

On \(\ker S\) the global scalar state form is proportional to \(I-uH+tR\), with \(H=\operatorname{Re}_HG\), \(R=\operatorname{Im}_HG\), \(t=\lambda/\mu\). The threshold for loss of positivity is an infimum of affine functions of \(t\). For a positive radiation Gram it increases, but the increase can be too small to measure. In two stored arrays a verified low-rank fit of \(R\) by receiver cross terms bounds its restriction to \(\ker S\) by \(7.56\times10^{-15}\) and \(3.03\times10^{-10}\) in the specified units. In one half-aperture layout the numerical threshold instead rises from 0.3521 to 0.7298 at \(t=10\). A reduced negative index alone need not leave a positive source residual. None of eight rescued historical parameter points yields a positive data bound. Local weights introduce a commutator with \(H\) and can increase the surviving source spectrum even at a fixed index. Causal ablation

Separation theorem. Assume a receiver-invisible vector makes the state form negative for every \(t\le T\). Suppose a current \(X\) fits the full measured data within tolerance, with \(-Q_o(X)\succeq0\) and \(Q_r^u(X)+TQ_o(X)\preceq0\). For \(t\le T\) a receiver-invisible perturbation of a fitting current makes any scalar quadratic constraint feasible. For \(t\ge T\), \(Xa\) is feasible for any unit coherent source \(a\). Hence every such scalar relaxation fails for all \(\mu>0,\lambda\in\mathbb R\). A joint certificate above the same tolerance proves strict separation. The constructed currents witness feasibility of the scalar relaxation and need no corresponding physical contrast. The conclusion does not bound the information available to a different one-source inverse method.

4. Constructive evidence, scaling and limitations

A one-sided Fourier bound makes the fine-grid proof practical while allowing all 9,216 contrasts to vary independently. Lowering the omitted symbol coefficients to a verified floor reduces the main inertia computation to at most 645 dimensions. For the two new full-aperture examples, the same dark witness covers all \(t\le10^4\). Two separate fits cover \(t\ge10^4\) with field residuals below \(8.56\times10^{-14}\). Joint lower bounds exceed 0.16832 and 0.11060 against noise allowances below 0.09097 and 0.08803, respectively. These are rigorous statements about the original fine-grid operators. This separation is stronger than a finite hyperparameter sweep. The data-selected multipliers and the counterexamples use only the operators and measured fields. Verification of the true materials took place after selection.

On the half-aperture array a spatially weighted optical identity adds a high-noise case. For the fixed weights proposed on the coarse grid, a two-channel Fourier enclosure proves \(K/\mu\succeq I/256\). A full-grid stationary residual argument then gives 0.6227832 against 0.5940488. Another spatial improvement on the coarse grid fails to transfer. The external-background principal submatrix gives a distinct negative result: across the audited family, its negative index alone exhausts the source dimension in all 112 regional trials. On real Fresnel 2005 data, three indefinite certificates have a positive lower bound, but each falls below the empirical error scale. At 4 GHz a simpler real-sector bound on the twin-cylinder data leaves 6.98 percentage points for an unknown physical-model discrepancy under the stated gain and noise assumptions. Regional evidence · Measured evidence

5. Publication scope

The strongest result is a computable, model-specific separation in noisy inverse scattering with finite receivers, supplemented by a spatial certificate and an obstruction to localization. A mesh-uniform or continuum transfer theorem with quantitative calibration control would strengthen its standing as an Inverse Problems article. A direct benchmark against a stronger single-source relaxation retaining simultaneous local constraints would clarify the computational price of the comparison. The present results establish neither an image-recovery advantage nor an unconditional lower bound for the measured objects. Reproducible experiments · Research handoff
