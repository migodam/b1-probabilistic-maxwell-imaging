Dear Editors of *Inverse Problems*,

Please consider “Material-set inversion with fixed constitutive traces and conditional scattering operators” for publication as a research paper. We ask what a finite, noisy scattering array can rule out about an inclusion, and when its existing measurements already determine that a further illumination will distinguish two material classes. The analysis retains the nonlinear current–field law of the two-dimensional transverse magnetic volume integral equation.

Two results connect the inverse and experimental questions. A change in the local dielectric law acts only on the current and total field at the affected cells. Eliminating their common positive complement preserves the inertia of an entire family of material and data tests, including singular cases. An independent argument gives a conditional bound on the full scattering operator at a fixed material class. Its source-side index identifies when any bounded receiving prediction is possible at saturation, and, for a prescribed incident-energy metric and error majorant, the incident fluence needed to remove the deficit.

We report a paired study of 48 base materials, a finite-core position study and a prospective comparison of four illumination policies. On a 9,216-cell grid the data exclude 247 of 256 possible core centres; a separate compatible example on the same grid leaves seven receiving directions uncontrolled despite 32 illuminations and full index saturation. For a narrower class, eleven coherent illuminations bound all 32 receiving directions; eleven ordinary angular illuminations at the same incident fluence leave five uncontrolled. Uniform illumination was as effective as the model-guided design in the realized first exposure of all six prospective examples. The paper reports the conditions for a guaranteed decision and the limits of what these data demonstrate, including the model-error margins in two microwave measurement collections.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.

The authors
