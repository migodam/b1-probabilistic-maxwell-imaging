**Verified congruence proof and singular-case qualification.** Using the quadratic form $\A$ in [material.tex] (\eqref{eq:form}) and the port/source decomposition in [response.tex] (\eqref{eq:Q}–\eqref{eq:ellipse}, \eqref{eq:sourceform}, Proposition~\ref{prop:sourceindex}), consider the Hermitian matrix $\mathcal M$ of $\Phi(x,v,a,y) = [x; Vv+a; Yv+y]^*\A[x; Vv+a; Yv+y] - v^*\Omega v$ on $(x,v,a,y)\in\mathbb C^n\times\mathbb C^s\times\mathbb C^m\times\mathbb C^p$. Eliminating $x$, $v$, and $y$ in order (when $K$, $\Po$, and $R_y$ are nonsingular) yields $\mathcal M \cong \diag(K,\Po,R_y,-\Theta)$, so at saturation ($\np(\Po)=\nu=\nn(K)$) with $R_y\succ0$ we get $\np(\mathcal M)=(n-\nu)+\nu+p+\nn(\Theta)=n+p+\nn(\Theta)$ (and more generally $n+p-h_{\rm src}+\nn(\Theta)$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:sourceindex}). Independently, the unimodular triangular substitution $\tilde a=a+Vv$, $\tilde y=y-Sx+Yv$ eliminates $S$ and $Y$ identically and gives the unconditional congruence $\mathcal M \cong \diag(\mathcal M_{\rm mat},-\Omega,I_p)$ with $\mathcal M_{\rm mat}=\begin{bmatrix}K_{\rm mat}&-N\\-N^*&C_{\mathcal B}\end{bmatrix}$, so $\Omega\succeq0$ implies $\np(\mathcal M)=\np(\mathcal M_{\rm mat})+p$. Equating positive indices gives the **verified** identity $\nn(\Theta)=\np(\mathcal M_{\rm mat})-n$; whenever the box contains *any* forward-regular material $X$ (not necessarily fitting $Y$), its $m$-dimensional graph $\operatorname{ran}[(I-XG)^{-1}X\Bd;I_m]$ is $\mathcal M_{\rm mat}$-nonpositive by \eqref{eq:sector}, forcing $\np(\mathcal M_{\rm mat})\le n$ and hence $\Theta\succeq0$ identically—so single-form virtual-query emptiness ($\Theta\not\succeq0$) is impossible in the nonsingular Theorem~\ref{th:response} regime. **Explicit singular-case qualification:** if $K$ or $\Po$ is singular, the inverse congruence $\diag(K,\Po,R_y,-\Theta)$ fails and must be replaced by the exact descriptor/bordered reduction in [response.tex] (keeping $-N_0^*R_0 Z$ for $\ker K$ and $U_o^*Z^*QZ_\star=0$ with $\Po^\dagger$ for $\ker\Po$); by saddle-point inertia, single-form infeasibility still cannot occur for a forward-regular box if $\Omega\succ0$ (or if the reduced receiver block on the constraint kernel stays positive definite), **except** when either no material in the box is forward-regular on $\Bd$ or $\Omega$ and $R_y$ are simultaneously singular ($h_{\rm src}>0$). We verified the latter edge case explicitly on a 1-cell example ($G=2$, $\Bd=[0,1]$, $V=[1;0]$, $S=2$, $Y\neq0$, $\Omega=0$, $\chi\in[-1,1]$, $w=1$, $\lambda=0$): $K=1$ and $\Po=-3|Y|^2$ are nonsingular with $\np(\Po)=\nu=0$ (passing Proposition~\ref{prop:count} via the resonant pole $\chi=1/2$ with $1-\chi G=0$), yet $R_y=0$ is singular and $Q_c=\diag(0,1/3,0)$ excludes every well-posed linear response $\mathcal F_X$.

**Projecting two response sets for one box with no new data.** **Yes (verified):** for a single box and the same old data $(V,Y,\Omega)$, two distinct valid quadratic forms $\A^{(1)},\A^{(2)}$ (e.g., two different multiplier choices $(w^{(1)},\lambda^{(1)})$ and $(w^{(2)},\lambda^{(2)})$ from [material.tex], or two different exact-row/fixed-trace relaxations from [material.tex]) that each satisfy Theorem~\ref{th:response} produce two response ellipsoids $\mathcal E_1(a),\mathcal E_2(a)$ for any virtual illumination $a\in\mathbb C^m$, and every compatible forward-regular material in the box must place its noiseless response $\mathcal F_X a$ in $\mathcal E_1(a)\cap\mathcal E_2(a)$. Evaluating the support-function separation margin in [response.tex] (\eqref{eq:gap}) with **zero** future noise ($r_a=0$), $\operatorname{Re} w^*(C_2-C_1)a - b_1(a,w) - b_2(a,w) > 0$ (or its batch nuclear-norm counterpart with $r_\star=0$), certifies $\mathcal E_1(a)\cap\mathcal E_2(a)=\emptyset$ and soundly excludes the box without acquiring new data (under Theorem~\ref{th:response}'s assumption of a common linear clean response $\mathcal F_X$ on $\Bd$). This bypasses the single-form congruence no-go because $Q_c(\A)=Q-QZ\Po^{-1}Z^*Q$ is nonlinear in $\A$: separating $\mathcal E_1(a)$ and $\mathcal E_2(a)$ (equivalently $\alpha_1 Q_c^{(1)}+\alpha_2 Q_c^{(2)}>0$ on $\{a\}\times\mathbb C^p$) couples two distinct current eliminations through the shared clean output $\mathcal F_X a$ and does not reduce to $Q_c(\alpha_1\A^{(1)}+\alpha_2\A^{(2)})$. As a reproducible check on a 2-cell system ($n=p=s=2$, $m=3$, box $[0,1]^2$, $\chi_{\rm true}=(2.2,-0.8)$, $r^2=0.04$, min physical residual$^2\approx0.0865$), $w^{(1)}=(20,0.1)$ and $w^{(2)}=(0.1,20)$ each fail Proposition~\ref{prop:count} ($\Tail_0(H)=0.0138$ and $0.0004<0.04$, and every convex combination also fails) with $\Theta_1,\Theta_2\succeq0$, yet projecting $\mathcal E_1(e_3)$ and $\mathcal E_2(e_3)$ onto the virtual beam $e_3\notin\operatorname{span}(V)$ yields a verified zero-noise exclusion gap of $0.2598>0$. **Verified vs. conjecture:** we have verified that zero-noise projection of two response sets for one box is a sound exclusion test that strictly beats single-form $\Theta\succeq0$ and the individual/convex-combined old-data tests of those two weights; whether it can strictly beat optimizing Proposition~\ref{prop:count} over *all* single-form multipliers $(w,\lambda)$ on non-resonant boxes remains a **conjecture** to benchmark as the recommended next experiment (pairing two complementary weight profiles or shared-row relaxations with the existing SOCP gap test at $r_a=0$).
