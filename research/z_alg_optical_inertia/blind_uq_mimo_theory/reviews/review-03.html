Targeted Critique of round3

1. Closed interval vs. continuous path. Verified: In region_intervals.csv and region_summary.json, 22/28 ROIs attain both endpoints \(0\) and \(4.5\) (exact closed hull \([0,4.5]\)), whereas only 5/28 certify a continuous feasible path across \([0,4.5]\)—1/22 on \(32^2\) versus 4/6 on \(96^2\). The theoretical distinction in THEORY_REFINEMENT.md is sound. Critique: The 4/6 continuous-path rate on \(96^2\) reflects mesh degeneracy of the peak functional \(p_R=\max_{i\in R}\chi_i\): spiking one sub-wavelength pixel (\(1/9216\) of the domain) to \(4.5\) barely perturbs 400 MHz scattering, whereas \(32^2\) single-pixel paths stall at \(0.75\text{–}3.75\) in 17/18 exact-hull ROIs. Also, roi_02_inner.json logs zero_or_base_ratio_upper: NaN because attainable.py applies Frobenius error eF entrywise so q0 straddles zero before sqrt() (the Bernstein check and base ratio \(0.107\) still validate the path—a reporting bug, not a proof flaw).

2. Shared-impedance \(w=0.05\) collision vs. all-pixel \(w=0.02\) disjointness. Verified: On the supplied \(6\times 6\) witness pair, mechanism_class_separation.json certifies all-pixel \(8\times 8\) disjointness at \(w=0.02\) under nominal and directional \(0.25\,\Omega\) calibration error. Conversely, mechanism_class_collision.json and mechanism_class_collision.npz verify a \(w=0.05\) pair colliding (\(\le 0.9903r\)) under one shared, passive, purely imaginary symmetric perturbation (\(D_a=D_b\), \(\|D\|_2<0.005\)); nominal collision stays unresolved (\(1.0033r\) at the 100-iteration cap). Critique: First, \(w\in[0.02,0.05]\) is \(\le 1.1\%\) of \([0,4.5]\) around one pre-supplied pair, not blind UQ. Second, class_separation.py bounds calibration independently per class (\(\|H_+\|_*+\|H_-\|_*\)), whereas class_collision.py shows shared-error shrinkage (\(0.013r\)) is \(\sim 9.5\times\) smaller than independent-error shrinkage (\(0.124r\)). Conjecture: Bounding shared-error difference sensitivity \(\|H_+-H_-\|_*\) will materially widen the certifiable calibrated separation radius beyond \(0.02\).

3. Alternative-array refits under grid mismatch and held-out errors. Verified: In summary.csv and cross_audit.csv, fixed A-compatible pairs fail on swapped-arc layouts B and C (0/7), refits satisfy at most one branch on B (3/7, patch-only) and none on C (0/7), and 28/28 refits fail both A (\(4.1\text{–}14.9r\)) and held-out ports (\(11.5\text{–}26.5r\)). Meanwhile, grid_transfer.csv shows \(32^2\leftrightarrow 96^2\) grid mismatch is \(9.1\text{–}15.7r\) on truth (\(2.0\text{–}8.1r\) on witnesses). Critique: Refit failures (\(1.1\text{–}6.6r\)) are local L-BFGS-B outcomes without an unconstrained-refit control or global infeasibility certificate—even patch fails 4/7 on B and 7/7 on C (conjecture: blind-ROI mismatch or optimizer stagnation). Because grid mismatch exceeds refit residuals and B-feasible refits still miss held-out ports by \(19\text{–}22r\), arc-switching breaks the specific A-trained pair inside a fixed discrete operator rather than proving continuum identifiability.

Practical Implication

Stop certifying \(1r\) discrete single-pixel peaks on \(32^2\); pivot to resolution-stable macroscopic functionals (e.g., coarse-block averages) under a combined noise-plus-discretization radius, pairing shared-calibration difference bounds (\(\|H_+-H_-\|_*\)) with unconstrained swapped-arc refit controls to separate physical identifiability from mesh artifacts and local-optimizer failure.
