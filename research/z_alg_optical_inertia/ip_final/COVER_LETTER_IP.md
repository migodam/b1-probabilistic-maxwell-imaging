Dear Editors of *Inverse Problems*,

Please consider “Certifying peak dielectric contrast from finite-aperture scattering data: optical power and negative inertia across illuminations” as a research paper. A low-contrast reconstruction with a large residual raises a practical question. Could a different low-contrast material fit the same readings? We give a deterministic test that can exclude all such materials from the data, independent of a reconstructed image or initial guess.

The mechanism depends on how much radiated power the receivers miss. A current in the null space of a half-aperture receiver can carry appreciable power, so optical balance restricts its ability to fit the data. In two densely sampled rings we bound this unobserved radiation close to zero. Across several incident waves the shared dielectric imposes a matrix constraint. We prove that each negative state direction can remove at most one term from the source-spectrum lower bound. On two 9,216-variable examples the joint bound excludes peak contrast at most 0.75, although every coherent single illumination fits the readings under both global laws and even has zero optimal discrepancy in the four-region semidefinite relaxation. Sixteen regions recover the exclusion with one coherent direction, delineating the separation.

We tested the transfer of fixed multipliers on 29 further simulated materials. The joint test excluded peak contrast at most 0.5 on all 22 objects of one archive and at most 5 on five of seven objects of the other. Data from two independent microwave chambers, Institut Fresnel and the University of Manitoba, quantify the effect of calibration and model discrepancy in field units. The chamber results report the remaining allowable error under explicit assumptions.

The paper connects inverse identifiability, source-space inertia and finite-aperture scattering in a form that can be checked on the native physical grid. We request double-anonymous review. Thank you for considering the manuscript.
