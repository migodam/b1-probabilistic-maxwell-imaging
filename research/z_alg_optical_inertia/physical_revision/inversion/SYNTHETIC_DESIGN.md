# Prospective electrically compact transition experiment

This is one new analytic material on a 12 × 12 grid. Its four observations are paired synthetic noise conditions. The physical question is whether the measured field can reject a broad contrast class while a bounded full-wave reconstruction remains in a predeclared ordinary discrepancy band. Choosing a compact target makes the spatially averaged dielectric contrast strongly observable in its outgoing field. Small spatial variations give a separate check on recovering the material rather than reducing the data residual alone.

- At 400 MHz and c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, use a square of side λ/6 = 0.125 m, with 10 equiangular unit plane waves and 20 equiangular receivers on a circle of radius 0.375 m. The held-out array rotates all transmit and receive directions by half the training angular spacing. Use exact full-wave scalar TM solves for all fits and tests on this discretization.
- Real contrast is a smooth rounded patch with a broad interior, gently raised and depleted lobes, and a thin air rim. Material formula is fixed in `compact.py`. Its shape is private during the fit. Both the low and released fits search the whole square and share update grids, data, initialization and budgets. There is no mask or support prior.
- Low cap is 0.5, released cap is 2. The same regularization and 20 / 30 / 44 objective-call slots as the original experiment apply with compact update grids 6 / 9 / 12. Use the same predeclared first-stage 2.5r discrepancy policy. Run all four conditions even when the test fails to rule out the class.
- Prospective radii are 0.45, 0.60, 0.75 and 0.95 times the noiseless difference between the generating material and the homogeneous square at the legal limit 0.5, evaluated prior to inversion by a full solver. Use the same prespecified pseudorandom complex unit direction (seed 2026112403), with the same small relative outward guard as the original problem. The uniform-limit square is a fixed design landmark and is not an estimated reconstruction. It sets the scale of a discrepancy experiment while leaving the actual fitted residual, lower bound, and materials unknown. No radius or sample is chosen after examining the fitted image. Report the actual relative noise and every outcome.
- Apply the same theorem using a fixed real-sector weight menu, with each weight proportional to the squared largest singular value of the public receiver matrix, at factors 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3 and 10. Keep all six tests. Allow switching only when the outward lower endpoint exceeds the original declared radius. Each weight uses the full 144-cell box.
- Independently enclose the same-grid forward residual of each selected map and the withheld synthetic-port error after writing the online outputs. The radius guards generation roundoff only. This construction illustrates a finite-model regime and does not characterize calibration or grid uncertainty.
