# Native full-ring array comparison

The physical claim is that adding independent illuminations can leave a positive source spectrum after the negative state directions have been discarded, subject to angular bandwidth at fixed electrical size. More surviving eigenvalues are necessary for this mechanism and a positive tail must still exceed the noise radius. A change in receiver sampling can also change the state inertia.

Six exposed independent synthetic materials are the unchanged native `optfresh_01`–`optfresh_06` arrays generated by `synthetic/generate.py`. The study pairs 400 MHz, 96², 2 m × 2 m, 3.75 m full ring at 1% and 2% noise with four source counts (10, 16, 24, 32) and two receiver counts (20, 32). All rings start at zero degrees. Existing `__pw360_10x20__pair1pct` and `__pair2pct` inputs are read directly. The stored 192² Green multiplier and its native restriction remain fixed. Native models use the stored binary64 arrays as exact represented coefficients.

For each array, each plane-wave amplitude is multiplied by `sqrt(10/s)`. At equal dwell and background impedance, its incident plane-wave power flux per unit length is proportional to amplitude squared. Consequently its sum over shots equals ten original unit-amplitude shots, or equal integrated fluence per unit reference aperture for this ideal illumination. Absolute antenna power or watts are unspecified. A receiver channel's gain is multiplied by `sqrt(20/p)` to keep the full-ring Frobenius norm in the same quadrature units; receiver weighting changes measurement and noise coordinates consistently. No extra receiving power is interpreted from this convention.

Each object's complex i.i.d. Gaussian draw is shared at coincident angular pairs. On pairs present in the original 10×20 array the pre-normalized direction comes from that object's original 2% RNG seed. Other pairs take fresh draws from a frozen master seed, 20261124031, with identical pair entries wherever selected. Each array's whole matrix is normalized once to 2% of its clean Frobenius norm and reused at half amplitude for 1%. Use the realized Frobenius radius and the inherited forward guard, 10⁻⁶ times the noisy Frobenius norm. Re-run the true-material ball audit only for newly generated observations.

The diagnostic cutoffs for effective incident and receiver singular ranks are relative amplitudes 10⁻¹, 10⁻², 10⁻³, 10⁻⁶ and the standard numerical machine rank. Physical degrees of freedom near 2ka are a bandwidth estimate, so this comparison does not assume a rank cutoff at that number. For the whole candidate family use the same multipliers at each cap and geometry, with no per-object tuning. The frozen native controls are the paper's stable scalar optical (mu=10⁻⁴, t=0.1), real-only object-01 baseline (mu=0.0004366418693307708), scalar optical object-05 baseline (mu=0.00035, t=0.2), quadrant optical (mu=10⁻⁴, normalized optical pattern 0.04, 0.16, 0.16, 0.16 as inherited), and the two previously verified fixed 4×4 profiles from `coherent_general_verified.json` with the exact binary normalized arrays. The paper's original 25×33 scalar grid is a separate, frozen numerical screening control. Test caps are 0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5, and 2.

The primary pass is a native outward-rounded distance lower bound above the public radius. When possible, verify all projected positives using the best available profile plus fallback, and verify near or representative negative cases at high caps. A strict upper distance of an individual bound is not material feasibility. The audited true materials are feasible upper witnesses, above all requested caps. A tested cap without a material witness and without a strict exclusion is unknown. If projected and native inertia differ, the native count governs. Proven `nu >= s` gives an algebraic zero for that multiplier; this and a zero positive tail with `nu < s` are separate diagnostic outcomes. No aggregate sample count multiplies the six materials by conditions.

The native scan first applies the inherited stable scalar optical profile to every array and cap. At each fixed p and cap, the original grid and frozen multiplier list supply near/crossing candidates for any still open case (projected ratio > 0.985), chosen by the number of unresolved rows each can address, with three attempts per row and up to 12 profiles per cap. The choice uses public data only. For additional high-cap controls, replay the projected scalar maximum at 32 sources, including cases with a negative maximum signed first retained eigenvalue. This selection is descriptive. The main scientific interpretation remains conditioned on the tested multiplier family.
