# Receiver-dark directions and the six full-ring scatterers

At a contrast cap of \(u=0.75\), one fixed four-quadrant weighting rules out the cap on three of the six scatterers at 1% noise. The same weighting has four negative current directions in every case. The object-to-object difference is in the measured coherent source spectrum after those four directions have been accounted for. The four current directions are nearly in the receiver null space and radiate weakly, but their outgoing constitutive residuals rule out interpreting them as free oscillations of the uniform cap medium.

## Six materials and the source spectrum

The native model uses a \(96\times96\) grid on a 2 m square, 400 MHz, ten unit-amplitude plane waves and twenty receivers on the 3.75 m full ring. The six materials were frozen before the optical tests. The 1% readings use the original 2% noise direction scaled by one half, and the 2% complex arrays are the original observations. Both radii include the original \(10^{-6}\|Y\|_F\) forward-solve allowance. The clean and Born fields in the table use the same native grid, acquisition and normalization. Here \(Y_B=S D_\chi B\), and the Born error is \(\|Y_c-Y_B\|_F/\|Y_c\|_F\).

The fixed weights have \(\mu_0=10^{-4}\), sector weight \(\mu_0\) throughout, and optical weights \((4,16,16,16)\times10^{-6}\) in row-major quadrants. These are the weights that gave object 04 its original 1% exclusion. The new results enclose the distance for these weights at both noise levels on every object. The rightmost column takes the largest lower bound among the previously verified global, four-quadrant and fixed sixteen-region choices. The union rules out the cap for 01, 04 and 05 at 1% and for 01 and 05 at 2%. The fixed profile alone gives \(d/r=1.256,0.519,0.936\) at 2% on 01, 04 and 05, respectively. A zero lower bound leaves feasibility undecided.

Four lobes and Double chevron have larger dielectric mass and stronger clean scattering than all three passing objects. In particular, Four lobes has the largest first source eigenvalue at 1% (5.6355). For each view let \(L=S^*Y+DB\), with \(D=\operatorname{diag}((u\mu_j+i\lambda_j)/2)\), and \(H_Y=Y^*Y-L^*K^{-1}L\). For the fixed weight the bound uses \(d^2=\sum_{j=5}^{10}[\lambda_j(H_Y)]_+\), with eigenvalues ordered from largest to smallest. The fifth eigenvalues for 01–06 are \(+0.010760,-0.021933,-0.022434,+0.002578,+0.006506,-0.022918\) at 1%. Object 01 also has a positive sixth eigenvalue. The positive contribution to \(H_Y\) from \(K\)'s negative directions concentrates at the top of the source spectrum. For 04 its first five diagonal contributions in the descending source eigenbasis are 4.027, 0.533, 0.237, 0.080 and 0.003. After the first four are removed, the balance between measured and accounted-for scattered energy determines the exclusion. For 04 the fifth source eigenvalue changes by only about 4.4% between 1% and 2%, whereas the squared radius grows fourfold. The six exposed materials establish this explanation for the measured views. Their scalar shape statistics give no transferable predictor.

## The four negative current directions

Write \(Z=D/\mu_0\) and \(\bar K=K/\mu_0=I+S^*S/\mu_0-ZG-G^*Z^*\). Native FFT actions give the first six eigenvalues as \((-0.104534,-0.058197,-0.046534,-0.003923,+0.077780,+0.081208)\). An independent interval check on the original stored propagator and weights gives exactly four negative eigenvalues, with a positive gap of at least \(0.03125\) in \(\bar K\). The four negative eigenvalues are separated, so the plot fixes the phase of each eigenvector by making its largest-amplitude pixel real and positive. For the equal-mean uniform weight control, two eigenvalues coincide at numerical resolution and the report compares their invariant space.

The receive map has rank 20. The worst-case null-space energy over all unit vectors in the four-dimensional negative span is 99.9923%. With the outgoing \(e^{-i\omega t}\) convention the equal-area disk kernel agrees with the stored stencil to \(9.1\times10^{-18}\) at the self and two neighboring offsets. For unit pixel-space vectors, \(R=(G-G^*)/(2i)\) measures radiated power in the model's Green normalization. A 720-angle integral from the outgoing Bessel kernel agrees with the quadratic form to about \(2\times10^{-15}\). After projection into \(\ker S\) and renormalization, \(v^*Rv\) remains 0.01740–0.03439. This is well below 1.95–2.08 on the four largest reactive \(G_H=(G+G^*)/2\) directions and well above \(2.3\times10^{-5}\)–\(1.4\times10^{-4}\) for four fixed random unit dark directions. The next two positive \(\bar K\) modes are comparably receiver-dark. The measurement null space and the sign of \(\bar K\) express different properties.

The outgoing constitutive defect stays above 1.084 for every unit vector in the four-dimensional span for the uniform \(\chi=0.75\) medium. The four largest reactive eigenvectors have principal angles of 48.6°–54.7° to that span. The uniform-cap solution driven by the ten incident fields, computed to a relative state residual below \(2\times10^{-11}\), has mean current gain 0.995 relative to \(\chi B\), and puts 21.2% of its energy in the negative span. The negative modes themselves have only \(3.5\times10^{-5}\)–\(6.5\times10^{-5}\) mean squared overlap with a normalized incident plane wave. In the six true media only 0.23%–1.60% of the full-wave current energy lies in that span. The modes place 21.5%–39.2% of their energy on the true supports, depending on mode and material. They are geometry- and weight-dependent directions poorly observed by the finite array. The test at one frequency and at a uniform cap establishes neither a physical pole nor the behavior of poles for all heterogeneous materials.

## Link to the ambiguous current in SOM and FFT-TSOM

Write \(P_{\ker S}=I-V_{20}V_{20}^*\), where \(V_{20}\) spans the receive row space. This projection gives the exact receiver-null current used here. The old solver's four-corner inverse DFT map uses \(\{-m_f,\ldots,m_f-1\}^2\) and projects away receiver singular vectors exceeding a relative threshold of \(10^{-3}\). All twenty singular vectors pass that threshold on this array. We compare spans using orthogonal projections. The inverse DFT coefficient metric of the actual solver is irrelevant to this comparison.

At equal rank four (\(m_f=1\)), the raw Fourier band captures 0.05%–0.84% per mode and its receiver-projected span captures 0.06%–1.00%. The principal angles between the two dark spans are 82.4°–89.6°. Sixteen Fourier columns capture 3.4%–23.5% per mode after projection, and 36 capture 41.5%–96.7%. At 64 columns the raw band captures 94.1%–96.6%, the receiver-projected columns capture 99.30%–99.36%, and the angles narrow to 3.2°–5.9°. The four directions therefore miss the earliest, small Fourier spaces and lie very nearly in a modestly enlarged standard Fourier space. Their weakness in the outgoing constitutive test and their low overlap with the six physical currents leave the proposed resonant-basis inversion experiment without its physical premise. [Original SOM](https://doi.org/10.1109/TGRS.2009.2025122), [FFT-TSOM](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAP.2010.2103027)
