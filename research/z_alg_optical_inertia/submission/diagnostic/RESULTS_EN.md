# A data-only check on an underfitted reconstruction

A peak certificate can reject a property of a reconstruction without reconstructing the material. We examined two previously exposed synthetic scalar-TM observations with existing interval-certified cap exclusions and ran the same ordinary nonnegative Tikhonov Gauss–Newton algorithm from the homogeneous background. The inversion minimized the full-wave least-squares objective with a fixed zero-reference ridge equal to one tenth of the largest squared singular value of the background Jacobian. Each step used the complete Jacobian and a full-wave acceptance test. The algorithm had no upper cap, and neither the stopping rule nor the penalty used the true material.

On the matched \(24^2\) ring (12 illuminations, 48 receivers), the seventh update met the relative-objective stopping rule. Its maximum contrast was 4.75615, below the certified threshold 5. Using the same stored observation and forward model, an independent interval evaluation placed its residual at 0.93413739, well above the CN99 radius 0.01051718. Thus a single forward check already rejected this reconstruction. The pre-existing cap-wide bound, 0.01469496, was itself above the radius and rules out **every** nonnegative material with peak at most 5. It is this universal statement that the pointwise residual cannot supply. An offline check placed the generating material inside the ball and confirmed a peak of 12.

For the native \(96^2\) half-aperture slotted block (10 illuminations, 20 receivers, 2% noise), updates one and two had peaks 0.22816 and 0.36992, with full-wave residuals 4.60773 and 4.24838. The third update crossed 0.5, and the prescribed 12-update terminal iterate reached 0.80150 with residual 3.46297. This run did not produce a below-cap terminal iterate. Its terminal residual remains outside the enlarged radius 0.10132681. The original native certificate sets a lower bound of 0.36886904 for every material with peak at most 0.5. The interval replay used the original stored Fourier operator. The experiment illustrates what a data-only exclusion establishes in addition to an ordinary residual check. It makes no claim of a more accurate reconstruction, a better data fit, or an advantage over a tuned optimization method.
