Dear Editors of *Inverse Problems*,

We submit “Locating dielectric inclusions and choosing a new illumination from finite-aperture scattering data” for consideration as a research article. The paper asks what a limited microwave array can establish about an inclusion before an image is known, and whether another exposure can decide between the remaining material specifications. The unknown material is shared by all the incident fields and multiple scattering is retained in the forward model.

Two results connect position and acquisition. A bordered-inertia test on local current and electric-field traces permits negative current directions and can be reused under signed changes of a material interval. For two material classes compatible with old noisy readings, a two-weight distance between conditional response sets bounds the power sufficient to distinguish the classes. A physical pair in the opposite direction bounds the power obtainable by any single-angle sequence at a fixed joint error budget.

On a full 96 by 96 field of view the method excludes 8,243 of 9,216 centres for an annulus with a weakly uncertain exterior and independently varying edge contrast. On four separate 32 by 32 examples a measured-null current rules out every nonzero choice of two nonnegative regional sector weights for the positive-state comparison. A conventional full-wave template fit located all eighteen simulated inclusions but produced a material in the noise ball for only one. Our calculation gives a verified full-wave example for each and a cover for all remaining centres. In independent illumination experiments the minimum simultaneous wave count is exactly two in one native case and exactly four in a smaller case. Classical coherent controls, unsuccessful location refinements, modelling errors across grids and held-receiver measurements from two microwave laboratories delimit the conclusions.

The manuscript includes the hypotheses of the results, the original-operator numerical bounds and a reproducible account of the synthetic and laboratory comparisons. Thank you for considering the submission.

The corresponding author
