## Judgment owned by Codex

The broad claim “find multiple nonlinear wave explanations, then choose a source to distinguish them” is not a defensible novelty claim. Root certification and upper-bound saturation are also established. The potentially publishable smaller object is a fully specified, finite-dimensional Maxwell ambiguity problem for which (i) every legal material explanation is computationally certified, (ii) physically reachable null/discriminating sources are characterized, and (iii) an explicit old-data noise set contains a certified material-Jacobian critical point that invalidates an assumed regular branch-tube continuation. Its value depends on a theorem and counterexample with reproducible physical coefficients, not a new method name.

R5 now supplies concrete evidence toward that object, but not a global originality certificate. Full historical papers and independent mature homotopy + the same coverage and source policy still require review. The known tools can reproduce the basic architecture; any specialized quotient advantage must be demonstrated in cost, guaranteed coverage under explicit structural assumptions, or a new stable inference result. No such overall noisy-recovery advantage is established here.

Journal fit is a research assessment: Inverse Problems is closest to this identifiability/critical-set focus; JIS needs a substantive imaging/algorithmic result; strong TAP needs calibrated electrodynamic application evidence. These are gaps, not predictions of editorial outcomes. Official scope context: [SIAM JIS](https://www.siam.org/publications/siam-journals/siam-journal-on-imaging-sciences/), [Inverse Problems guidance](https://publishingsupport.iopscience.iop.org/journals/inverse-problems/). TAP scope page retrieval was unsuccessful; no quoted policy attributed to it.
