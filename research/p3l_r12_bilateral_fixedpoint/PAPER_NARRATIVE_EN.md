# Bilateral Modes, Feasibility Barriers, and Finite-Noise Material Exploration in Nonlinear Maxwell Imaging

## Working abstract

Nonlinear electromagnetic inverse scattering admits data-compatible material explanations whose usefulness depends on material diversity and task coverage rather than candidate count alone. We investigate whether hierarchical bilateral observability analysis improves such exploration without explicit high-order Maxwell derivatives. Starting from an exact fixed-host volume-integral formulation, we express the material Jacobian through receiver and illumination factors and their cellwise modal products. The complete bilateral Gram contains exactly the information of the corresponding full Jacobian. Re-decomposing an already selected singular subspace introduces no new information; only a changed physical state can supply a new sensitivity structure. We implement prior-aware material directions, first-order transverse correction, single-refresh and feedback-driven variants, and ordinary randomized full-coordinate optimization under shared resource limits. Four previously exposed, difficult P1 development cases show genuine sensitivity changes but no improvement in the prescribed material-neighborhood coverage. A separate nine-digit native-material benchmark exposes a boundary-feasibility failure of unconstrained weak predictors. A declared box-retracted revision generates nonlinear Maxwell candidates, yet hierarchical online actions remain at their initial reconstructions. Ordinary additional Gauss–Newton optimization reduces average material error, while none of the evaluated digit-eight clouds recovers both holes. These controlled results identify redundant hierarchy and representation constraints without claiming posterior calibration, exact continuous fibers, or population generalization. Stronger imaging claims require a mechanism that improves material coverage beyond matched full-dimensional randomized inversion.

## Contribution status

The exact identities, modal factorizations, Newton continuation and randomized optimization have established precedents. The present evidence supports an implemented, bounded comparative study and several structural limitations. It does not yet support a new superior Maxwell imaging algorithm or a strong IEEE TAP originality claim.

Proposed sections: exact model and material measure; bilateral factorization and fixed-point distinction; feasible predictor–corrector algorithms; exposed P1 comparisons; native digit reconstructions and topology; actual costs and failures; unresolved global material coverage.
