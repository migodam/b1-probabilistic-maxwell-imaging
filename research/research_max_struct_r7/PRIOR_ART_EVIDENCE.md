**Specific q-small port-Gram attribution:** after Schur/Woodbury reduction to q selected scalar cells, expanding the finite rational numerator into a finite feature vector and precontracting its measured-port norm into a small Gram is elementary least-squares algebra. Replacing a port-size loop by that precomputed quadratic form is a legitimate amortized engineering claim only after setup, q-dependence, stability and candidate count are charged. It is not a first-discovery claim or, by itself, an inverse algorithm. DCTMC already explores nonlocal intermediate representations; parametric rational/ROM literature already compresses transfer responses. A defensible R7 distinction needs useful material-action selection and physical outcomes beyond these ingredients.

## What remains potentially distinctive, without a first-discovery claim

1. A-specific multi-affine Möbius coordinates and a complete coupled conditional/MM implementation can be an algorithmic contribution if they retain useful recovery and reduce total cost. The elementary coordinate geometry and general surrogate-descent principle are prior art. Neither a smaller fixed-current loss nor fewer inner calls proves improved physical inversion.
2. A residual-aware finite-action generator plus stable, counted Schur evaluation can be useful if it reaches physically verified endpoints outside ordinary local search at equal actual work. Standard resolvent/Woodbury algebra or one improved two-cell update does not establish that claim.
3. A Maxwell-compatible LSL field lift under incomplete polarization/ports would need its own assumptions, calibration, error analysis, and experiments. The closest LSL/LUCIE prior art must be compared directly; LNO is a representation option, not evidence of this capability.

The present measured pilot supplies no positive novelty or A-superiority evidence: all three pipelines have identical saved physical endpoints for each measured target. Preserve that negative result.
